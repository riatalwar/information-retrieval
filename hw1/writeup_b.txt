Features
What didn't work well:
  - Length of whitespace at beginning
  - "-" for list items
These features, though I thought they might be useful for recognizing non-plaintext or list segments, did not end up being particularly helpful.

Good features:
Counts:
  - Number of alphabetical only words and number of numeric only words
  - Check for notable symbols such as @, ", \, /, |, _
  - Look at comma counts, also checking how many follow numbers versus letters
This category of features is useful for identifying notable characters or word formats associated with the various categories. High counts of alphabetical-only words is indicative of plaintext, while numeric "words" are more likely to belong to things like tables or perhaps signatures. Some symbols are commonly associated with signatures or graphics and the counts can be used to distinguish them from plaintext. Lastly, comma counts are indicative of plaintext patterns when sparsely used after alphabetical words, but are more likely to indicate tables when following numbers or used as a different kind of separator when used frequently.

Recognizable patterns:
  - Numeric list items
  - Phone numbers
  - Emails
  - ">" or ":" to indicate quoted
Certain recognizable patterns are highly useful for identifying quoted segments, as the > or : are strong predictors. Numeric list items are harder to pick out, but there are several different common formats that we can account for. Phone numbers and emails are also commonly used in signatures, and sometimes headers, so recognizing the most common formats of those is an informative feature.

Metadata:
  - Previous line prediction
  - Relative doc positioning (percentage of way through the doc)
I modified some of the original structure of the code to provide the relative positioning of each line within the document (detecting new documents based on the standard email header). This helps to identify whether a line is likely to be in certain categories, as headers are typically at the top, signatures at the bottom, and plaintext somewhere in the middle. In addition, I used the previous line prediction as another feature because lines typically come in groups. This is an interesting feature, as it is also possible for incorrect predictions to exacerbate errors, but it led to an overall improvement in accuracy.

Other useful information:
  - Count large blocks of spaces
  - Average word length
  - Distance between first alphabetical character and first non-whitespace character
  - Character distribution: percentage of alphabetical characters in first vs second half of text
These final characteristics are helpful primarily for identifying graphics, as many spaces, short words, long periods without alphabetical characters, and uneven character distribution are often indicative of graphics. To a certain extent, these features can also apply to signatures and tables, further differentiating them from plaintext, which typically has a fairly even space and character distribution.
